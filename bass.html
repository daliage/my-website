I started playing bass around 2022, mostly for fun. However, After a while I decided that a DIY bass kit would be a fun project to make. So, I did some research and settled on the Solo SRBK-15. It sat on a table for a while before we did anything with it, but eventually my dad and I went to Home Depot to buy some stain and finish and then got to work. It was a lot of sanding, watering, staining, and finishing, but it looked great.

After the stain dried, I began to work on the electrical components. I put the pickups in, began to put the pre-wired knobs into the body, and then a wire disconnected. I didn't know where the wire was supposed to connect to and I couldn't find the correct wiring diagram online, so like any mediocre electrical hobbyist, I decided to redo all the wiring myself. The wires it came with weren't that good and it couldn't be that bad, right?
THIS IS FORESHADOWING

Anyway, I found a good diagram online and got to work. It was a huge hassle trying to solder at weird angles to get the pickup wires on there, but eventually I got it to work. I plug my bass into my amp, and got nothing but a loud buzzing. Occasionally, I would get a small amount of noise, but nothing crazy.

Rather than troubleshoot, FORESHADOWING AGAIN I decided that the best course of actions would be to try the wiring over again with a different wiring diagram. The schematic I found was weird and kinda odd, so it probably was just messed up. I got back to work, found a new wiring diagram, and got to more painstaking soldering, only to strike even less success with absolutely no actual sound coming from my bass (other than more buzzing).

I forget how many times I redid the wiring, but I decided to actually do something instead of redoing everything. I got the jack, got a cable, grabbed a multimeter, and checked the pins. The cable has two connection points: hot and ground. I checked for those on the pins of my bass' output jack. Tip worked fine, but ground wasn't working. The output jack had three pins: tip, ground, and one for a 9V battery if the bass had active pickups. My bass was passive, so I wasn't using that pin, or so I thought. This whole time, I was soldering ground to the battery pin, which is why the output wasn't working. Finally, I soldered the correct ground, and everything worked.

Anway, enough backstory. Time for the fun stuff.
